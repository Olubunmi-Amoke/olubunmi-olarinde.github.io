About Me

I’m a Machine Learning Engineer and Data Scientist with a recently completed M.S. in Data Science (Eastern University, July 2025). I build data-driven systems that move from exploration to deployment, combining applied machine learning, research thinking, and product-oriented development.

My work spans machine learning, NLP, computer vision, and analytics, with experience building end-to-end projects that translate messy real-world data into usable tools and insights. I’m especially interested in systems that operate in high-stakes or human-facing contexts, where technical decisions shape trust, outcomes, and adoption.

Recent projects include the Smart Sickle Cell Diary, a Streamlit application that uses LLM-assisted insights and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) to support structured reflection and self-management, alongside projects in classical ML, computer vision, and applied analytics.

Across my work, I care deeply about how models are trained, evaluated, and deployed, not just whether they perform well, but whether they behave responsibly in context. I enjoy roles that sit at the intersection of engineering, research, and product, where rigorous thinking meets practical impact.

Beyond data science, I’m also a vocal coach and musician. I enjoy bringing creativity, communication, and teaching into technical spaces, including once running a summer music camp for kids ages 6–13 (equal parts chaos and joy).

This site features selected projects, my ethics capstone The Model Doesn’t Speak My Pain, and ongoing technical work
