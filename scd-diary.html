Project Overview

People living with Sickle Cell Disease (SCD) often face unpredictable, intense pain episodes. Traditional trackers can be rigid or unintuitive. This project enables quick daily logging (free-text + light structure) and uses NLP/ML to surface useful, actionable insights, supporting self-awareness, proactive self-management, and clearer conversations with care teams.

What’s new (Aug 2025): The app now prioritizes trigger detection and supportive, context-aware recommendations over raw prediction scores.

Evolution of the Project

  • Initial Focus (v1): Predict Pain Level (regression) and Emotion (classification) from diary entries; visualize pain–mood patterns.
  • New Direction (v2 – Current): Detect potential triggers (e.g., cold exposure, dehydration, stress, exertion, infection cues) from natural-language logs and provide supportive insights & recommendations (hydration/warmth/rest strategies, journaling prompts, provider-ready notes). Charts remain, but numeric predictions are de-emphasized in favor of actionable guidance.

Approach (Current)

  • Data: No longer limited to synthetic logs, now includes entries from a mini‑pilot with real SCD patient testers (de‑identified for privacy). Prompt‑generated supportive suggestions are still derived from curated documentation assembled for RAG reference.
  • Pipeline: The system still applies light text preprocessing to normalize inputs before running trigger detection and generating supportive insights. These functions are coordinated across the main application (streamlit_app.py), RAG helper routines (rag_integration.py), and trigger detection logic.
  • App UX: An Insight Card summarizes triggers and next‑best actions; lightweight charts provide optional visualization for reflection. Users can also download their log entries as CSV files for personal record‑keeping or to share with providers. Daily logging remains a quick ~2-5 minute process.

Results & Ethics

The app improves reflection by surfacing likely triggers and practical next steps rather than only showing scores. Ethical considerations (interpretability, controllability, and bias awareness) guide the design to support more equitable ML-driven insights.

⚠️ Beta Notice: Research prototype for information only; not medical advice. Real-world validation and co-design with patients/clinicians are active next steps.
